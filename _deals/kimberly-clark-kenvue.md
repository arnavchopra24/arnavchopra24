Kenvue sells Tylenol, Listerine, Neutrogena and Band-Aid. Johnson & Johnson spun it off in 2023. In November 2025, Kimberly-Clark, the maker of Huggies and Kleenex, agreed to buy it for about $48.7 billion including debt. The deal closed on Nov. 2, 2026.

The headline price was $21.01 a share. Kenvue shareholders didn't get $21.01.

## Why the price changed

Most of the payment was in Kimberly-Clark stock: $3.50 in cash plus 0.14625 of a Kimberly-Clark share. The day the deal was announced, Kimberly-Clark's stock fell 14.6%, to $102.27. When the deal closed a year later it was at $108.25, so each Kenvue share was worth about $19.33.

{% include bar-chart.html chart=page.charts.price %}

That is still a 35% premium over Kenvue's $14.37 price before the deal. But it's well short of the 46% in the headlines.

## What Kimberly-Clark paid

The price works out to 14.3 times Kenvue's EBITDA over the previous 12 months. If all the expected synergies show up, that drops to 8.8 times. Kenvue shareholders now own about 46% of the combined company.

## What could go wrong

Kenvue's sales were flat in 2024 at about $15.5 billion, and it faces lawsuits tied to Tylenol and baby powder. To win approval in Europe, Kimberly-Clark agreed to sell its Kotex brand there. It has also set up a $3.1 billion integration program that runs through 2029.

## My take

I think Kimberly-Clark overpaid. A 46% premium and 14.3 times EBITDA is a lot for a business that wasn't growing, and the synergies have to do most of the work. Its shareholders seem to agree: a year later, the stock was still about 10% below where it was before the deal.

The brands are excellent, though. If the cost savings arrive on time, I could be wrong.

## What this deal taught me

- When a buyer pays in stock, a falling share price lowers what the seller receives too.
- A premium that looks huge on signing day can shrink by closing day.
